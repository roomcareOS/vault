# Marketing Claims Bank (Intervooh)

The marketing rulebook for [[Intervooh]]: positioning, an evidence-backed claims register, and hard guardrails. **Rule of use (verbatim, load-bearing):** *any claim in public copy must trace to a row in the claims bank or be pure product description. Tier C numbers may be paraphrased directionally but not quoted in paid advertising without re-verification.* The evidence itself lives in [[Research - Intervooh Evidence Base]].

## Positioning

**The unit is your specific interview.** Everyone else sells practice-in-general or a question bank; Intervooh takes *company + role + date* and hands back a day-by-day programme. Tagline:

> **Tell it the interview. Arrive ready.**

Defensible because the 2026 competitor set (Yoodli, Big Interview, Final Round AI, Interviewing.io, Google Interview Warmup) is all practice tools, courses and copilots — none offers a date-shaped programme as the core unit. Re-verify competitor pricing before any launch copy; pricing moves.

## The claims bank (the useful pattern)

Fourteen approved copy lines, each stored as: **copy line → evidence → source link → required qualifier**. Highlights:

- Structured interviews are the strongest predictor of job performance — and trainable (Tier A, no qualifier needed).
- Mock-interview practice linked to far better offer rates in randomised trials — but must stay generally phrased; never quote "9×" (trials were in specific populations).
- Practising out loud beats re-reading; spacing beats cramming (both Tier A).
- The "140 applications per vacancy" UK statistic must be date-stamped — it will age.
- Product-true claims (no citation needed): the three refusals, the rubric, Success Profiles and NHS values mapping.

## The ethics wedge

2026 is the year live-copilot cheating went mainstream (~22% of candidates admit it) and employers responded with verification rounds. The three refusals are therefore both ethics *and* efficacy. Campaign-ready contrast:

> *"There are two kinds of interview AI now: the kind that cheats for you, and the kind that makes you good. We only build the second."*

## Hard guardrails (keep wording tight)

- **Never** say or imply: "reads your confidence", "detects nerves", "analyses your body language/emotions", "AI that knows how you feel" — these describe an EU-prohibited category in recruitment (AI Act Art. 5(1)(f), fines to €35M/7% of turnover) and are unsubstantiable.
- **Never** guarantee outcomes ("get the job", "double your offer rate"). Use "linked to", "in randomised trials, participants…".
- **ASA substantiation** (UK ad regulator): every objective claim needs held evidence — the claims bank is that evidence register; keep access dates.
- **Population honesty**: the strongest RCTs are in populations facing employment barriers; general-population phrasing stays qualitative.

## Do-not-claim list

- "9× more likely to get an offer" (real stat, wrong population for general marketing).
- "Speakers with fewer fillers get 30% higher credibility ratings" (blog-tier provenance).
- "The only app with [X]" for anything except the date-shaped programme unit — re-verify at each launch.
- Competitor pricing without a fresh check.
- "Backed by science" as a bare tagline — always link the specific claim to the specific study.
